# 11-research-background-generator

> Research background construction, argument flow, problem framing, gap
> development, and academically defensible introduction writing.

## 1. Purpose

This module helps construct and review the research background section.

It does not exist to generate generic academic paragraphs.

Its purpose is to ensure that a background section logically connects:

    phenomenon
        ↓
    evidence
        ↓
    problem
        ↓
    previous research
        ↓
    limitations
        ↓
    research gap
        ↓
    research objective

Priority:

**argument quality \> evidence \> logical flow \> writing style**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Background Principle

A research background should answer:

1.  What phenomenon exists?
2.  Why does it matter?
3.  What evidence supports this importance?
4.  What problem remains?
5.  What has previous research attempted?
6.  What limitation remains?
7.  Why is the current research needed?

Do not write a background as:

    technology development
    +
    generic importance
    +
    researcher interest
    +
    method introduction

without evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Background Structure

## Section 1: Broad Context

Purpose:

Introduce the domain and phenomenon.

Requirements:

-   factual;
-   relevant;
-   supported by appropriate sources.

Avoid:

-   generic openings;
-   exaggerated importance.

Bad:

> In this modern digital era, technology is developing rapidly.

Better:

> Recent advances in natural language processing have enabled automated
> analysis of large-scale textual data across multiple domains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section 2: Specific Problem

Move from general context to the actual problem.

Explain:

-   what limitation exists;
-   who or what is affected;
-   why existing approaches are insufficient.

The problem should not be invented only to justify a preferred method.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section 3: Evidence From Previous Research

Summarize relevant studies.

Do not create a paper list.

Use:

    Study pattern
    +
    finding
    +
    limitation
    +
    implication

Example:

> Previous studies have explored transformer-based classification
> models, but evaluations have often been limited to specific datasets
> and domains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section 4: Research Limitation

Identify what remains unresolved.

Possible forms:

-   insufficient comparison;
-   limited dataset;
-   unclear generalization;
-   missing evaluation;
-   inconsistent findings;
-   practical limitation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section 5: Research Gap

A valid gap requires:

    existing evidence

    +

    unresolved issue

    +

    reason it matters

    +

    ability of current study to address it

Avoid:

> No research has used method X.

unless verified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Section 6: Research Objective

The objective should directly respond to the gap.

Bad:

> This research aims to contribute to technology development.

Better:

> This study evaluates whether method X improves classification
> performance compared with baseline methods under the same evaluation
> protocol.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Anti-Generic Background Rules

Avoid:

-   "in today's era";
-   "rapid technological development";
-   "very important";
-   "has a significant impact";
-   "many researchers have studied";

unless followed by specific evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Claim Strength Control

Use wording according to evidence.

Strong:

> demonstrated

when evidence supports it.

Moderate:

> suggests

when interpretation is involved.

Limited:

> may indicate

when uncertainty remains.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Method Introduction Rule

Do not introduce a method before explaining the problem.

Wrong flow:

    Transformer is powerful.
    Therefore this research uses Transformer.

Better:

    Problem exists.
    Previous approaches have limitations.
    Method X may address this limitation.
    This study evaluates that possibility.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Verification:

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Background Quality Gate

Before finalizing:

-   [ ] Problem is clearly defined.
-   [ ] Claims have evidence.
-   [ ] Literature supports the argument.
-   [ ] Previous research is synthesized.
-   [ ] Research gap is evidence-based.
-   [ ] Objective follows from the gap.
-   [ ] Method is not introduced without justification.
-   [ ] No generic filler dominates the section.
-   [ ] No unsupported importance claim exists.
-   [ ] Conclusion of the background does not exceed evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Writing Workflow

Use:

    Literature evidence

    ↓

    Argument map

    ↓

    Background draft

    ↓

    Evidence audit

    ↓

    Writing audit

    ↓

    Final version

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Final Principle

A strong background does not say:

> This technology is interesting.

It demonstrates:

> This problem exists, evidence shows the limitation, previous work is
> insufficient in a specific way, and this research addresses that
> unresolved issue.
